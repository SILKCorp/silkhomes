A Day in the Life

Structure exists. Autonomy is paramount. Here's what a typical day might look like.

The Core Principle

Your time is yours. Community activities are optional. We provide rhythm, not requirements. Show up for your commitments, handle your responsibilities, and otherwise - do your own thing.

A Sample Day

AM
6:00 - 7:00 AM
Early Morning (Optional)

Some residents rise early for personal practice - yoga, meditation, journaling, or a walk. The house is quiet. Coffee is available in the shared kitchen.

This is personal time. No one monitors when you wake up.

AM
7:00 - 8:30 AM
Breakfast & Morning Routine

Make your own breakfast in the shared kitchen. Clean up after yourself immediately. Some days there might be a shared breakfast if someone's on cooking duty - attendance is optional.

Shared kitchen etiquette: Clean as you go. Label your food. Respect others' items.

AM
8:30 AM - 12:00 PM
Work Block

For Crew: This is when you handle your responsibilities - housekeeping shifts, garden work, property maintenance, guest turnovers. Check your schedule the night before.

For Students/Residents: Remote work, study, personal projects. The homes have reliable WiFi. Your bedroom is your office if needed.

Crew duties are scheduled in advance. You manage your own time to complete them.

PM
12:00 - 1:00 PM
Midday Break

Lunch is self-serve. Some people eat together, some alone. Weather permitting, the garden is a nice place to take a break. Check on plants if you're on garden rotation.

PM
1:00 - 5:00 PM
Afternoon Block

Second work block or personal time depending on your schedule. Crew members often have afternoon tasks - guest communications, shopping runs, prep for next day. Remote workers continue their projects.

Need to run errands? You manage your own transportation and time.

PM
5:00 - 7:00 PM
Evening Transition

Wind down work. Some people exercise, take walks, or spend time in the garden. This is the most social time if you want it - porches and common areas see more activity.

Still not required. If you want to stay in your room, that's fine.

PM
7:00 - 8:30 PM
Dinner

Most nights: Make your own dinner in the shared kitchen.

Some nights: A crew member on cooking rotation prepares a shared meal. Attendance is optional but appreciated. These happen 2-3 times per week typically.

Shared meals use communal ingredients. Label personal food clearly.

PM
8:30 - 9:00 PM
Wind Down

Cleanup from dinner. Last kitchen use before quiet hours. Check schedules for tomorrow. Brief check-in with housemates if needed (not required).

Night
9:00 PM onwards
Quiet Hours Begin

The house settles. Conversations move to whispers or personal spaces. No TV in common areas. Reading, personal calls in your room, or quiet activities only.

Quiet hours are 9 PM - 8 AM. This is non-negotiable.

Weekly Rhythm

Structured Elements
  • Weekly house meeting (30 min max) - logistics only, not mandatory social time
  • Shared meals 2-3x per week - optional attendance
  • Garden check-ins if you're on rotation - assigned in advance
  • Crew schedules posted weekly - you manage your time within those commitments
Unstructured Elements
  • Your personal practice - yoga, meditation, exercise
  • Social time - as much or little as you want
  • Weekends - unless you have crew duties, they're yours
  • Evenings - after duties are done, time is yours

Seasonal Variations

Spring
Spring

Garden prep intensifies. Longer daylight means more outdoor time. Rain days for indoor projects.

Summer
Summer

Peak garden season. Early mornings to beat heat. Porch time in evenings. More travel visitors.

Fall
Fall

Harvest and preservation. Preparing homes for winter. Beautiful foliage. Cooler nights.

Winter
Winter

Indoor focus. More indoor project time. Snow management duties. Cozy house time.

What This Isn't

Not Like This

  • Mandatory morning circles or group activities
  • Required attendance at meals or gatherings
  • Someone tracking your whereabouts
  • Pressure to socialize if you don't want to
  • Group processing or emotional check-ins
  • Communal decision-making for personal choices

Actually Like This

  • Adults managing their own schedules
  • Clear expectations, delivered independently
  • Respect for privacy and personal space
  • Optional community for those who want it
  • Brief, efficient coordination meetings
  • Individual responsibility for individual matters
