Grown from a Commune, Built on Community

The roots of SILK Homes run deep, inspired by a childhood on a communal farm and a vision for sustainable, connected living.

The Founder's Vision

SILK Homes is inspired by its founder's upbringing on a 100-acre family commune, where ten houses shared resources, labor, and life. We carry that spirit forward by creating a network of homes built on mutual support and a love for the land.

That early life taught us a fundamental lesson: that sharing resources and skills doesn't diminish them, it multiplies them. SILK Homes is our way of bringing that communal spirit into the modern world.

Our Philosophy

We believe in peaceful living, self-sufficiency, and finding beauty in the historical. We thrift for materials, giving new life to old treasures. Every property has its own victory garden, providing fresh produce and a connection to the earth.

Why thrift? Because we believe our homes should have soul. Every piece of furniture, every repaired fixture, tells a story. It's a sustainable choice and a rejection of the disposable.

Our Victory Gardens are more than just a source of food. They are a classroom, a quiet retreat, and a tangible connection to the seasons and the land.

Our Homes

Our portfolio is a collection of character-rich townhomes, each on about a quarter-acre of land. We are proud stewards of four homes built in the 1800s, four from the 1950s, and several more modern dwellings. Each one is a canvas for our hospitality crew to maintain and cherish.

We don't just rent houses; we cultivate a community network, much like the one I grew up in.

The Vision Forward

SILK Homes represents more than accommodation—it's a return to intentional living. In a world that often feels disconnected, we offer spaces where people can slow down, connect with the land, and experience genuine hospitality.

Our guests, primarily hardworking medical professionals, find respite in our peaceful homes. Our crew members discover not just employment, but a lifestyle that aligns with values of sustainability, craftsmanship, and community care.

This is our invitation to you: to be part of something larger than yourself, to help steward these historic homes, and to build the kind of community we all wish existed in the world.
